## Introduction

For this lab, you will demonstrate your understanding of what you've learned in the course by creating and delivering a short, engaging presentation about a chosen cybersecurity topic. For context, you will assume the role of a cybersecurity professional working for an imaginary company. Therefore, the presentation content should target ***internal customers*** within the organization (non-technical audience) and explain a relevant security topic in clear and accessible terms.

## Part one: Choose your security topic

For this exercise, your task is to review the topics you've learned in the course so far and consider the following:

- Which topics are particularly relevant to organizational security?
- What's your comfort level regarding explaining these topics to a non-technical audience?

Here are some topic ideas to help get you started:

- CVEs.
- The importance of timely software updates.
- The principle of least privilege.
- The importance of proper configuration of Windows security settings.
- Attacks, threats, and vulnerabilities.

> 💡 Before proceeding to the next step, take a moment to write a summary of your chosen security topic and its relevance using your preferred note-taking application. This will help you clarify your ideas and provide you with some initial content to build upon.

## Part two: Research your chosen security topic

The goal of this exercise is to research your chosen topic. Please ensure you focus on the following aspects:

- The topic's ***definition*** and key concepts.
- Real-world ***examples*** of security incidents or risks related to the topic.
- ***Recommendations*** on how to mitigate associated risks.
- How the topic affects organizational security and operations.

> 💡 Before moving on to the next step, revisit your notes and summarize your findings into a presentation outline below your initial security topic summary. Identify the most critical points you want your audience to grasp. Additionally, record any relevant data, statistics, or visual aids you may want to include.

## Part three: Craft an engaging presentation

It's time to put it all together! For this exercise, you will begin building your presentation deck with content based on your research. Here are some recommendations on how to structure your slides:

- **Title/cover**: This slide should include your topic and name.
- **Introduction**: Why is this topic important?
- **Main content (3-4 slides)**:
  - Includes a clear explanation of the topic.
  - Provides real-world examples or scenarios.
  - Lists implications for the organization and its employees.
  - Recommends actions that can be taken immediately to mitigate associative threats.
- **Conclusion**: Recap key points and share actionable takeaways.

You should be able to deliver your presentation in under 3 minutes. To support this, be sure to limit bullet points to 3-5 per slide and write ***speaker notes*** to accompany each slide; this will help you practice delivering your presentation.

> 🏆 Tips: Focus on keeping your content simple. Remember, your audience will be non-technical. Avoid jargon and overly complex explanations. Also, use simple, visually appealing slides with minimal text; include visuals like diagrams or images to enhance engagement.

### Tips and tricks for a successful presentation

Here are a few pointers to consider before presenting your work:

- **Know your audience**: Remember to keep your explanations clear, concise, and jargon-free.
- **Practice**: Perform a few "dry runs" of your presentation to ensure a smooth delivery.
- **Speak clearly and confidently**: Add pauses and pace yourself to avoid rushing through the content.
- **Get creative**: What can you do to make your presentation more engaging? Consider using appropriate humor, storytelling, or real-world examples to illustrate your points.

This lab is an excellent opportunity to apply what you've learned, improve your presentation skills, and build confidence in communicating cybersecurity topics effectively.  Good luck!

## Part four: Deliver your presentation

Finally, record yourself delivering your presentation. You can use a video conferencing tool like Zoom to do this. Remember, your presentation should be no longer than 3 minutes. Be sure to focus on clarity and simplicity.

After you've recorded your presentation, add the recording to your presentation deck as the last slide. Finally, submit the slide deck.
